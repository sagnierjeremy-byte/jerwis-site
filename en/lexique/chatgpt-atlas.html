Browser agent · OpenAI · 2026

ChatGPT Atlas / Operator

ChatGPT Atlas (spun out of the Operator project) was OpenAI's web browser with a built-in AI agent: you told it what you wanted ("book this table", "buy this product"), it took over, opened the sites, clicked, and filled things in for you. It was reserved for ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers.

OpenAI shut this browser down on August 9, 2026, after announcing it on July 9. Its features moved into the ChatGPT desktop app and a ChatGPT extension for Chrome. For today's AI browsers, see AI browser.

— What it is

ChatGPT Atlas / Operator, in plain words

In plain words: it was Chrome, the OpenAI edition, except an AI was at the wheel of the browser and did the web tasks for you while you did something else.

In practice, I would tell it: "find the 3 cheapest flights Paris-Marseille for Friday, put them in a table, and book the cheapest one if it's under €80." Atlas opened the sites, compared, and booked if the condition was met. It asked me to confirm before paying.

Like Perplexity Comet, but with OpenAI's tech behind it (the "Computer Use" model that sees and interacts with any web interface).

What I use it for

The cases where I pulled out Atlas / Operator:

  • Automated e-commerce shopping: fill the cart on 3 sites, compare, finish the order.
  • Bookings: restaurant, flight, hotel, doctor. Atlas navigated the site, filled the fields, and I confirmed.
  • Batch applications: if you're hunting for freelance gigs or a job, it can fill out 20 similar forms.
  • Price tracking: "check this site every day, ping me if the price drops under €100."
  • Deep competitive watch: a tour of 30 sites with structured info pulled into a table.

How it compares

vs Perplexity Comet: Comet is way more affordable ($20/month vs $200/month). Atlas was stronger on complex actions (payments, multi-step flows).

vs Genspark: Genspark bets on deliverables (slides, docs, calls). Atlas bet on pure web action.

vs Manus / Chinese agents: Manus showed impressive demos but stays in limited access. Atlas was productized and stable.

What it costs

Prices as of May 2026 (source: openai.com/chatgpt/pricing):

  • ChatGPT Plus: $20/month: limited access to Atlas / Operator
  • ChatGPT Pro: $200/month: full access to Atlas, heavy usage, premium models
  • Business: from $25/user/month: for teams

Atlas shipped as a standalone browser (Mac, Windows). The download was free, but without a Pro subscription the agent was very limited.

My take

In spring 2026, it was the most technically advanced one out there. When Atlas works, it does things the others can't (completing a purchase from A to Z, handling a multi-step booking).

The catch: $200/month is steep for occasional use. And the agent is still slow (often 2-3 minutes for a task). You could do the same thing faster by hand in 10 minutes of work.

I used it for the repetitive tasks where the time saved justified the wait. For everything else, Comet at $20/month did the job just fine.

Quick questions

Does Atlas still exist?

ChatGPT Atlas was OpenAI's web browser, born out of the Operator project, with a built-in AI agent that ran full web tasks (booking, buying, filling out forms) on your behalf. OpenAI shut it down on August 9, 2026, and its features moved into the ChatGPT desktop app.

Can the agent pay without asking?

No, manual confirmation is required by default. You can set the thresholds in the options.

Does my data go through OpenAI?

Yes for agent sessions, see OpenAI's privacy policy. A "Temporary Chat" option is available.
