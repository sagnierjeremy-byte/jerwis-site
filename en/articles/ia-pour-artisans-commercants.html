Why now, and why you?

In 2025, 26% of French small businesses were using artificial intelligence — a number that had doubled in a single year, according to data available as of July 2026. And by March 2026, 47% of small and medium-sized businesses had already launched at least one AI project, up from 29% at the start of 2024.

This isn't a trend reserved for big tech companies. It's hitting plumbers, florists, independent consultants, and shop owners — people who don't have a spare moment, let alone two hours to write a quote or figure out what to post on Instagram this Thursday.

I'm not a developer. I don't code. And yet I use AI every single day for tasks that would have eaten up a big chunk of my schedule two years ago. What I'm going to show you here is how to do the same — no special training, no big budget, no jargon.

The use cases are specific: quotes and admin work, product pages, social media, and customer review responses. Four areas where AI can deliver immediate, measurable results.

One useful note before we dive in: when I talk about generative AI, I mean tools that can produce text, ideas, or rewrites based on an instruction you give them. If you want to understand what's running under the hood, check out the glossary entry on language models.

Quotes and admin: the time you never see coming

Writing a quote usually looks the same every time: you pull up a template, swap out the line items, rephrase the terms, fix the typos. Multiply that by ten quotes a month and you've got a real chunk of time — for a result that's often nearly identical each time.

AI can automate or dramatically speed up this kind of repetitive work: drafting emails, following up with clients, generating quotes from a template. You give it the key details — what the job is, the price, the timeline, the terms — and it structures the document. You review, tweak, send.

Here's how I'd approach it in practice. You create a prompt (an instruction) that describes your business and the quote format you want. You paste in your raw information. The AI drafts it. You approve it. The whole thing can take under five minutes where it used to take twenty or thirty.

ChatGPT, in its standard or Enterprise version, is often the go-to for this kind of task. Claude Opus 5 — available as of this article's publication — has a strong reputation for nuanced writing, which can be useful if your quotes include carefully worded commercial language.

For client follow-ups, same idea. You describe the situation ("client hasn't replied in 10 days, quote for $900, professional but warm tone") and the AI drafts the email. If you want to go deeper on this, I have a dedicated article on writing your emails with AI.

The trap to avoid

Never send an AI-generated quote without reading it first. The tool can invent a deadline, drop a condition, or rephrase a price in a way that's ambiguous. Reviewing it is still your job — AI handles the heavy lifting, not the final check.

Product pages: 95% less time

If you sell products — in a physical store, on a website, or on a marketplace — you know how time-consuming product pages can be. Finding the right words, describing the specs, matching the tone, avoiding repetition when you've got fifty items to cover.

The numbers on this are striking: writing product pages with generative AI can cut the time compared to manual writing by up to 95%. For large catalogs, that can mean up to 80 hours a month reclaimed.

80 hours. Per month. That's a full working week.

The method is simple. You give the tool the raw specs for the product: material, dimensions, use case, target audience, price. You specify the tone you want (technical, accessible, warm). The AI writes a complete page. You can then ask it to spin off a shorter version for social media, or an SEO version with targeted keywords.

For the visuals that go alongside those pages — banners, posters, promotional materials — AI can help there too. I wrote an article on creating a logo, poster, or flyer with AI if you want to explore that angle.

In all these cases, the quality of your instruction is everything. A vague prompt gives a vague result. If you want to understand how to write instructions that actually work, the article on writing a good prompt without being a developer is a solid starting point.

Social media: posting without burning out

Keeping Instagram, Facebook, or LinkedIn fed on a regular basis when you're running a business solo or with a tiny team is a constant effort. Coming up with ideas, writing captions, adapting the format for each platform, tracking what works.

AI tools can take on a big chunk of that work: spotting trends, adapting content to each platform, writing captions, automating responses to common questions. ChatGPT is often used for caption writing and post idea generation.

Marketing teams using AI for their content reclaim between 4 and 6 hours per week, according to data from April 2026. For a solo operator managing everything themselves, that's significant.

In practice, you can ask the AI to generate ten post ideas based on your topic, then write the captions for each one. You pick what fits, tweak the tone if needed, and schedule. What used to take half a day can fit into an hour.

Google Gemini, connected to the Google ecosystem in real time, can be useful if you want to cross-reference search trends with your content strategy. Perplexity AI — which handled over a billion queries per month in 2026 and has 45 million users — is also used for research and finding editorial angles.

My takeaway

AI doesn't replace your voice. It unsticks you when you're staring at a blank page. Use it to generate a first draft, then inject what makes it yours: an anecdote, a detail from your trade, a turn of phrase that sounds like you. That's what separates a generic post from content that actually connects.

Customer reviews: reply fast, reply well

89% of consumers read responses to customer reviews before making a purchase, according to 2025 data. That number alone should be enough to convince you that responding to reviews — positive and negative — isn't optional.

The problem is that writing a personalized, professional, non-robotic response takes time. Especially when you're getting several a week and juggling everything else at the same time.

AI can generate suggested responses to customer reviews, taking the context into account (positive review, negative review, with or without details). You give it the review you received, specify the tone you want to strike, and it proposes a response. You adjust if needed, and you publish.

For a negative review, this is especially useful: AI can help you craft a response that acknowledges the issue without being defensive, and shows you're taking it seriously. That kind of well-written response can turn a negative review into proof of professionalism in the eyes of future customers.

For the common questions you get — by email, DM, or through your website — AI can also help you build template responses that you personalize from there. It's a light form of automation, no complex tool required, just a good prompt and a bit of organization.

Where to start, concretely

A lot of people I talk to hesitate to get started because they don't know where to grab the thread. Here's how I'd approach it if I were starting from scratch.

Pick one task to start with. Not AI for everything at once. Pick the task that costs you the most time and is the most repetitive. For most people, that's writing emails or responding to reviews. That's a good entry point.

Test a free tool first. The free version of ChatGPT already gets you pretty far. Claude also has an accessible free tier. No need to subscribe to everything before you know what works for you. To help you choose based on your use case, I have an article on how to choose your AI for writing, coding, or creating images.

Learn to write clear instructions. That's the core skill. An AI tool is only as good as the instruction you give it. No technical jargon needed — just precision and context.

Automate gradually. Once you're comfortable with one tool for one task, you can expand. If you want to go further with automation without touching any code, the article on automating your tasks with AI without coding will give you concrete ideas.

On cost: consumer tools like ChatGPT or Claude run under $25/month in their paid versions. ChatGPT Enterprise is more suited to larger organizations. For a freelancer or small business, the standard version covers most use cases.

On privacy: be careful about what you paste into these tools. Avoid putting in personally identifiable data about your clients (names, contact details, contract numbers). Work with anonymized or fictional data when you're building your prompts.

FAQ

Do I need to be a tech expert to use AI in my business?

No. Tools like ChatGPT or Claude work in plain language: you write what you want, the same way you'd explain it to a colleague. No code, no technical training. The only skill that matters is writing clear instructions — and that takes a few hours of practice to pick up.

What does it cost to bring AI into a small or solo business?

Most tools offer a functional free version. Paid tiers (ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro) typically run around $20–25 per month. For most people who use them regularly on time-consuming tasks, that pays for itself within the first week.

Can AI fully replace a human for writing or customer relations?

No, and that's not the goal. AI produces a first draft, suggests phrasing, proposes responses. But you're the one who reviews, adjusts, and signs off. For customer relations especially, the human eye is still essential — particularly for sensitive situations or negative reviews.

How do I protect my clients' data when using third-party AI tools?

Avoid pasting personally identifiable client data into these tools. Work with anonymized examples. Read the terms of service for whatever tool you choose, especially around how your data is used for model training — some tools let you turn that off.

Where do I actually start when bringing AI into a small business?

Pick one repetitive task that costs you time. Test a free tool on it for a week. Measure the time saved. Then expand gradually. Don't try to automate everything at once — small-step adoption is far more effective.

Does AI work for all types of trades and small businesses?

It's useful anywhere there are repetitive text-based tasks: quotes, emails, descriptions, review responses, social media content. An electrician, an esthetician, a restaurant owner, a consultant — all of them can benefit. Highly manual or physical work doesn't benefit directly, but the admin side of that work absolutely does.

My takeaways

AI isn't a magic tool. It's a work tool — like good invoicing software or a shared calendar. It saves time on the tasks you repeat without thinking, and it frees up space for what actually matters in your business.

What convinced me was seeing concrete numbers: 95% time saved on product pages, 4 to 6 hours reclaimed per week on content, 89% of consumers reading review responses. These aren't abstract promises — they're documented use cases, from 2025 and 2026.

I test all of this myself first. And what I see is that the tradespeople and freelancers who get started don't go back. Not because it's revolutionary, but because it's simply more efficient.
