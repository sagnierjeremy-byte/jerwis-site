Your budget
under the microscope
without touching your passwords

Scared of getting your bank logins stolen but still want to use AI to get clear on your spending? I'm exactly in that boat. I tested several ways to analyze my budget, spot the leaks, clean up my subscriptions, and build a savings plan without ever connecting my accounts to anything. Here's what actually works in November 2026.

  • Hunt down forgotten subscriptions through manual entry or OCR scanning

Why I refuse to link my accounts

I'm neither a developer nor a cybersecurity expert. Just a curious entrepreneur who refuses to get left behind by AI. My money deserves healthy caution. I do all this for myself first. I want to know where every single euro goes, without risking my bank logins ending up in some database outside my control.

Privacy comes up constantly among finance pros. Many worry as much about data security as they do about AI output accuracy. Me, I solve the problem at the source. I don't connect anything. I keep total control over what I share. If you want to know more about protecting your financial data, I laid out my whole approach in an article on personal data privacy.

How I analyze my spending without a bank connection

The method is simple. You download a CSV file from your online banking portal. It's a standard export. No need to hand over your passwords. Then you drop that file into a tool that reads and sorts everything automatically.

Tally Assistant works exactly like that. You upload a CSV or snap a photo of a receipt. The AI categorizes your spending on its own. No direct bank connection needed. For paper receipts or PDF invoices, tools like ExpenseBot and ReceiptsAI use text recognition to pull out the amounts and sort them. You stay in control of your data.

I also test quick manual entries. AI Money lets you dictate a purchase or send a photo. You can even log a transaction through WhatsApp. Handy when you're out and about and want to record a expense before you forget.

What I check before every upload

Before dropping a CSV file into ChatGPT or Claude, I delete the useless columns. I keep the date, the label, the amount, and the category. I strip out full card numbers and IBANs. Even if I trust the tool, I'd rather limit the attack surface to the bare minimum. It's a habit that takes ten seconds and changes everything.

To guide the AI properly through this analysis, it helps to know how to write solid prompts. And if you want to go further with automating repetitive tasks, I explain how to automate tasks without writing code.

Where forgotten subscriptions hide

That's often where the AI surprises me most. When you feed it three months of statements, it spots the repeating lines. A forgotten subscription becomes visible right away. It's not magic. It's just pattern detection applied to your own data.

The other day, I found out I was still paying for a photo storage app I hadn't opened in eight months. The AI caught it because the amount came back on the same date with an almost identical label. I could have spotted it myself. But across hundreds of lines, the human brain skips over what it thinks is unimportant. The AI doesn't make that mistake.

AI assistants help you structure your income, your bills, your goals, and your timelines, explain budgeting approaches, and suggest tweaks without ever asking for your bank logins. You give them a handwritten summary or an exported spreadsheet, and they highlight the patterns you miss.

Build a custom savings plan

Once you have a clear picture of what's coming in and going out, you can ask the AI to simulate plans. You tell it your income, fixed bills, variable spending, and your goal. It suggests ways to adjust. It's a structuring exercise, not an absolute prediction.

Richify uses AI agents for this kind of work. You punch in your numbers by hand. The tool stays privacy-first. You don't share any bank identifiers. The AI cross-references your data and shows you scenarios.

I tested a plan to build an emergency fund this way. The AI suggested shifting some annual payments to monthly installments to smooth out cash flow. It wasn't some earth-shattering revelation. It was just putting in order what I already knew but didn't dare look at.

The mistake that gave me a bogus projection

I briefly believed a savings projection because the AI had forgotten to count an annual subscription. Since then, I always have the AI double-check the plan while spelling out annual and quarterly expenses. A model doesn't have your memory. You need to hand it every piece of the puzzle.

The limits you should know

AI helps you structure and visualize. It doesn't replace a financial advisor. It can also get an amount wrong, mix up two labels, or invent a pattern that isn't there.

The AI market for personal finance has been growing fast since 2025. Companies in the sector are adopting these technologies big time. But on the user side, caution still matters. Never blindly follow a savings or investment suggestion generated by a model. You alone are responsible for your decisions.

I'll add that I used AI to structure some of the analysis I'm talking about here. It's a tool. Not an oracle.

FAQ

Is it really safe to use AI for my finances without connecting my bank accounts?

Yes, as long as you never share your passwords, full card numbers, login IDs, or banking password. You stay in control by importing CSV exports yourself or dictating your transactions. For the extra cautious, a local model running on your computer with Ollama or LM Studio analyzes your data without ever touching the internet.

What kinds of data can I give an AI to help me manage my budget?

You can hand over CSV files exported from your online banking, receipt photos, manual summaries of your income and spending, or spreadsheets you built yourself. Always avoid giving out your full IBAN, card numbers, security codes, or banking passwords.

How can AI find forgotten subscriptions if it doesn't have live access to my bank statements?

You give it your statements as text or a file. It scans the lines to spot identical amounts that keep coming back at regular intervals. It's recurrence analysis on the data you feed it. It doesn't need real-time access to do that job.

Can I use ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude to analyze my spending and create a savings plan?

Absolutely. You can open a CSV file directly in the Claude desktop app or in ChatGPT in Work mode. Gemini 3.8 Flash is available online. These models can read spreadsheets, categorize spending, spot duplicates, and suggest savings splits. Spell out your goals and constraints in the prompt to get something useful.

What are the risks and limits of using AI for budgeting without a bank connection?

The main risk is hallucination. The AI can calculate a wrong total, mix up an annual expense with a monthly one, or suggest an unrealistic plan. It doesn't have access to your actual tax situation or contractual constraints. Use it as an organizer, not a certified financial advisor.

Are there free AI apps that follow this no-bank-codes approach?

Yes. Tally Assistant, Richify, AI Money, and similar apps offer budget tracking without any bank link. Some let you scan receipts, others work through manual entry or voice dictation. Open-source models running on your computer are also free to download and work offline.

My takeaways

AI helps me see my budget differently. Not because it's smarter than me, but because it doesn't sleep and it skips zero lines. I keep my bank passwords to myself. I only give it what it needs to analyze. It's a simple balance. And it works.
