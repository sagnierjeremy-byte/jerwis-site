Free AI
or a €20 subscription?
What I learned testing all three

Wondering if that €20 a month for ChatGPT Plus, Gemini Advanced, or Claude Pro is really worth it when a free version exists? I asked myself the same question a few months ago, watching my dashboard and invoices pile up. Today, I'm sharing what I learned by actually testing each plan, zero developer jargon, with one goal only: helping you make the right call for your business without paying for hot air.

8 min read Level Beginner Tools ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude
In 30 seconds

What you'll walk away with

  • The three leaders all offer subscriptions around €20 per month in 2026
  • Paid plans mainly unlock much higher usage limits and advanced features
  • ChatGPT Plus, Google AI Pro, and Claude Pro each target a different ecosystem
  • A subscription is only worth it if you use it regularly for concrete professional tasks

Three subscriptions, three philosophies

Right now, three plans are fighting for your wallet. ChatGPT Plus runs about €23 per month in France, VAT included, from a base price of $20. This subscription gives you access to GPT-6.1 Sol, the model OpenAI launched on September 29, 2026, which you'll find in ChatGPT's Work mode.

On the Google side, the plan is now called Google AI Pro, formerly Gemini Advanced. It gives you access to Gemini 4 Argon, Google's new flagship model, opened in the Gemini app on October 13, 2026 and not included in the free plan, with a context window that goes up to one million tokens. The fast model in the lineup is now called Gemini 3.8 Flash.

Finally, Claude Pro is priced at $20 per month before tax, or $17 a month if you pay yearly. With Pro, you get Claude Opus 5.5, released September 22, 2026, and Claude Sonnet 5.5, released September 28, 2026. The biggest one, Claude Fable 5.1, is included in the Max plan (from $100 a month) and paid with usage credits on Pro.

Each of these three plans starts from a different vision. OpenAI bets on a complete ecosystem where you generate images, analyze files, and create custom assistants. Google builds on what you already have: Gmail, Docs, and Sheets. Anthropic, with Claude, prioritizes reasoning quality and advanced team collaboration tools. None is intrinsically better. It all depends on the tool you already use every day.

One thing's for sure: pricing is comparable everywhere. We're hovering around twenty euros or dollars per month. For an entrepreneur, it's not a huge amount, but it's not negligible either if you stack multiple subscriptions without a strategy.

What paying really changes

The most immediate difference is usage volume. With free ChatGPT, you get about ten messages every five hours before the system downgrades you to a lighter model (F3). Upgrading to ChatGPT Plus, that ceiling jumps to about one hundred sixty messages every three hours with standard models (F3). For daily professional use, this isn't a detail: it's the difference between a tool that's available and a tool that cuts you off mid-thought.

At Claude, the ratio is similar. The Pro version allows at least five times the usage of the free tier, with caps between fifty and one hundred messages every five hours (F8). Again, if you spend your day iterating on strategies or analyzing documents, the free tier quickly becomes friction.

But paying isn't just about more messages. It's mainly access to features that turn AI into a colleague rather than a gadget. ChatGPT Plus unlocks image generation, file analysis, Deep Research tools, custom GPT creation, and an Agent mode (F10). Claude Pro opens Claude Code, the developer tool, and a Deep Research mode (F8). In the desktop app, Claude can also work inside a folder on your computer that you hand it (this used to be Cowork, which is merging into the regular conversation in late 2026). As for Google AI Pro, it natively integrates Google Workspace (F6) and gives you access to Google's flagship model.

There's also the size of documents you can send. The context window, meaning the amount of text the model can process in one go, now reaches one million tokens on the biggest models, like Gemini 4 Argon or Claude Opus 5.5. Even if you don't know what a token is, remember this: it lets you hand it a fifty-page report and ask for a structured summary in one pass.

Free or paid: daily life

In practice, what does a day look like with either?

On the free plan, you ask a question, you get an answer, then you wait. You can't upload your client contract PDF to have it proofread. You can't generate the image for your next post. You're working with an assistant that has a short memory and gets tired fast.

On a paid plan, AI becomes a workstation. You send it your Excel database, ask it to spot inconsistencies, draft an email from your Google Calendar, or prepare a research summary from twenty articles. Agentic workflows — these processes where AI chains several actions without constant intervention from you — are reserved for subscriptions. ChatGPT Plus's Agent mode or Claude Code are concrete examples.

For a non-developer entrepreneur like me, the line is clear. The free tier is for testing, validating a use case, getting your feet wet. The paid tier is for scaling up. When I'm prepping a product launch, I need to iterate ten times on the same document. With the limit of ten messages per five hours imposed on free ChatGPT users (F3), I'd be blocked before even finalizing my structure.

It's also about flow. I don't want to count how many messages I have left before I can relaunch an analysis. I want to ask my question, get an answer, adjust, repeat. That freedom of pace is well worth the twenty euros a month when you use it regularly for work.

Multimodal capabilities also change the game. Generating an image or a video, or analyzing a scanned PDF file in one go, saves you time right away. Free gives you text. Paid gives you documents, images, and workflow.

My advice

Don't subscribe to all three at once. Pick the one that already touches your main toolkit. If you live in Google Workspace, test Google AI Pro. If you want a Swiss Army knife with images, files, and agents, start with ChatGPT Plus.

Who is it worth it for?

I'm not going to tell you everyone should pay. If you ask three questions a week to draft emails or check spelling, stick with the free version. You won't get more value by spending money.

On the flip side, a subscription becomes relevant if you recognize yourself in these situations: you regularly analyze long documents, you prepare structured commercial proposals, you do intensive research, or you need to generate visual content quickly. In these cases, the ability to process hundreds of messages without interruption (F3, F8) and to load complex files changes the game.

The cost, about twenty euros a month, is less than one billable hour to a client. If AI saves you even just one or two hours a month on mechanical tasks, the investment pays for itself. For my part, I do all this for myself first. I pay because I'd rather delegate repetition to a machine and keep my strategic thinking time intact.

What really matters is consistency. A one-month subscription during a big project can be enough. You don't need to pay all year if you only use the tool three weeks per quarter. The flexibility of these plans lets you adapt spending to your actual activity, not a fear of missing out.

I'm thinking especially of entrepreneurs running their business solo. When you don't have an administrative assistant, paid AI becomes tangible support. It doesn't replace humans, but it lets you push through heavy files without getting scattered.

The trap to avoid

Paying doesn't replace a good prompt. I've seen €20-a-month subscribers get worse results than free users who master the art of prompting. Before upgrading to paid, make sure you know how to write a good prompt. A subscription unlocks capabilities, not magic.

How I'd choose

If you're hesitating, here's how I'd go about it.

First criterion: your ecosystem. If your business runs on Google Workspace, Gmail, and Google Calendar, Google AI Pro integrates natively into your existing tools (F6). You don't have to change habits. If you're more agnostic and want a tool that does a bit of everything — text, image, file analysis, custom agents — ChatGPT Plus offers the widest range to date (F10).

Second criterion: the nature of your tasks. For complex reasoning on long documents, Claude Pro excels. Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 are built for that. For work on codebases or agentic processes, Claude Code is a serious asset (F8). If you need to process extremely long documents, the one-million-token context window of Gemini 4 Argon or Claude Opus 5.5 is a real technical advantage.

Third criterion: privacy. Before sending sensitive data, check the vendor's policy. Claude Pro offers interesting options on that front. I invite you to read my article on data privacy to understand the risks before throwing your client contracts into an interface.

Fourth criterion: your volume. If you regularly hit the free limits, you know it. The message suggesting you come back later or the automatic downgrade to a lighter model are clear signals (F3). At that point, paid isn't an option anymore — it's mandatory to stay productive.

Beyond the subscription

Upgrading to paid is good. Getting the most out of that subscription is better.

The first thing to do is learn how to communicate well with AI. An expensive subscription will never make up for a fuzzy prompt. If you don't know how to phrase your request, you'll get mediocre results no matter what's under the hood. I wrote a simple guide on how to write a good prompt when you're not a developer.

Then, explore the features you've unlocked. If you pay for ChatGPT Plus and don't use Agent mode or custom GPTs (F10), you're leaving part of your investment on the table. If you're on Claude Pro, try the Claude desktop app on a folder from your computer to automate repetitive tasks. These advanced tools take time to learn, but that's where the real competitive advantage lies.

Also remember you can use several AIs for free while paying for only one. I often keep an eye on competitors' free versions to compare answers on strategic questions. Paid becomes my main tool, free my backup tool. This hybrid approach lets me choose the most suitable AI depending on the mission type: writing, data analysis, or visual generation.

FAQ

Is a free AI version enough to start your business?

Yes. Most free tools let you test the real value for your job before spending a dime. Start there, identify your recurring uses, then upgrade to paid when you hit a concrete limit.

What are the most frustrating limitations of free versions for professional use?

Message caps. With free ChatGPT, you quickly hit the limit of ten messages per five hours before switching to a lighter model (F3). At Claude, space is even tighter. For continuous work, these interruptions break the flow.

Can you cancel or switch back to free anytime?

Yes. These subscriptions have no long-term commitment. You upgrade, downgrade, cut according to your projects. Don't hesitate to adapt your subscription to your actual activity rather than paying out of habit.

Is your data safer with a paid subscription?

Not automatically. Each vendor has its own policy. Claude Pro offers enhanced privacy options. Before sending sensitive info, read my article on data privacy to make an informed choice.

Are there cheaper alternatives to €20 per month?

Free versions remain the best alternative. Then, some specialized tools you already use — like Notion or Canva — include AI credits in their subscriptions. But to access cutting-edge models like GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon, or Claude Opus 5.5, you need a paid plan, which starts at $20 a month at both OpenAI and Anthropic.

How do you know if you often hit the free limits?

If you see messages suggesting you "come back later" or if the tool systematically downgrades you to a lighter model, that's the signal. With ChatGPT, it's switching to a "mini" model after ten messages (F3). When that happens several times a week, it's time to consider subscribing.

My takeaways

I pay for an AI subscription because it saves me time on tasks I repeat every week. It's not a tech luxury, it's a productivity tool. At about twenty euros a month, the cost is quickly recouped as soon as AI replaces one hour of mechanical work.

But I stay vigilant: I reassess every quarter whether I'm actually using what I pay for. Because the best AI isn't the one with the biggest model. It's the one you actually use, the one that integrates into your workflow without friction. If you have to choose, start with the essentials. One well-used subscription is better than three underused ones.
