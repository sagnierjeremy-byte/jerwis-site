Why slides were eating my time

For a long time, building a presentation took me half a day. Not because I was short on ideas — but because I spent most of that time fiddling with margins, fonts, and alignment. The actual message came last.

I'm not a designer. And I'm not the kind of person who's going to spend hours learning PowerPoint shortcuts. What I want is for my idea to look good, fast, without the layout swallowing my afternoon.

That's where AI comes in. And the difference is real.

According to data from March 2026, 47% of French SMBs had already launched at least one AI project — up 18 points from a year earlier. That number says something important: AI isn't just for big companies anymore. It's showing up in the day-to-day of entrepreneurs. And presentation creation is one of the first places you feel it concretely.

The idea is simple: you describe what you want to present, the tool generates a structure, picks a layout, and all you have to do is refine the message. It's not magic — there's still work involved — but the starting point changes completely.

Gamma AI: from idea to slides

Gamma AI is a platform built to create presentations, documents, and web pages from a simple prompt, an outline, or an imported file. You type an idea in a few words, the tool generates a structure, suggests visuals, and delivers something presentable in minutes.

The workflow looks like this:

  1. You enter your idea — a sentence, a bullet-point outline, or you import an existing document.
  2. The AI generates a structure — it breaks the topic into sections, suggests titles, and fills the slides with content.
  3. You customize — you edit the text, swap the visual theme, rearrange the blocks.
  4. You share — via a direct web link, or you export to PDF or PowerPoint.

What struck me the first time was the speed. In under five minutes, I had a ten-slide presentation on a topic I'd sketched out in three lines. Not perfect, but structured, readable, and visually consistent.

What it costs. Gamma offers a free plan with 400 lifetime AI credits. Each generated presentation typically uses around 40 credits — which gives you roughly ten full presentations without spending a dime. Beyond that, paid plans unlock unlimited AI usage.

The limit you need to know. When you export to PowerPoint, Gamma's dynamic layouts can get flattened into static images. In practice: if you open the .pptx file to edit it slide by slide, you might end up with locked blocks that are hard to tweak. If you know you'll need to edit the file afterward, plan for that upfront.

The trap to avoid

If you're delivering an editable PowerPoint file to a client or colleague, test the export before you commit. Gamma shines when you present directly from its web interface or export to PDF. The editable .pptx is its weak spot.

To get the most out of Gamma, the quality of your initial prompt makes all the difference. I have an article on how to write a good prompt when you're not a developer — the same principles apply here.

Copilot in PowerPoint: AI where you already work

If you're already using Microsoft 365, Copilot plugs directly into PowerPoint. No tool-switching required. You open PowerPoint, click Copilot, and describe what you want.

Copilot can build a presentation from a text description, but also from a Word file or an existing document. You hand it a brief or a report, and it generates the corresponding slides — text, layouts, and visuals included.

In July 2026, Microsoft integrated Black Forest Lab's FLUX.2 Flex model for image generation. That means visuals produced directly in PowerPoint via Copilot are now sharper and more customizable — no need to hunt for photos on an external site.

What it costs. Copilot isn't available on every Microsoft 365 plan. You need a Microsoft 365 Premium plan (for individuals) or an eligible business plan. If you're already subscribed to Microsoft 365 for your work, check your subscription tier before wondering why the Copilot button isn't showing up.

The main advantage. Everything stays inside the Microsoft ecosystem. You work directly in the .pptx file — no export, no conversion. If you need to collaborate with colleagues on SharePoint or Teams, this is the smoothest option.

The main limit. Copilot is less autonomous than Gamma when starting from scratch. It excels when you already have material — a Word doc, a report, a structured brief — and you want to turn it into slides. For going from a vague one-sentence idea to a full deck, Gamma is usually more responsive.

For choosing between different AI tools depending on what you want to produce — text, images, presentations — I wrote a comparison article on how to pick your AI based on the task.

Gamma or Copilot: which one should you pick?

The question isn't "which one is better" — it's "which one fits your situation."

Criteria Gamma AI Copilot in PowerPoint
Starting point An idea, a sentence A document, a brief
Ecosystem Independent, web-based Microsoft 365
Free plan Yes (400 credits) No (subscription required)
Editable .pptx export Limited (layout flattened) Native
Image generation Yes Yes (FLUX.2 Flex since Jul. 2026)

If you're starting from zero and want to try without paying: start with Gamma. If you're already in the Microsoft 365 ecosystem and have documents to turn into slides: Copilot makes more sense.

My takeaways

I use both depending on the situation. Gamma to quickly prototype a new idea. Copilot when I already have a structured document and want to stay in PowerPoint. Both save me from spending time on layout — and that's the whole point.

Other tools worth knowing

Gamma and Copilot aren't the only options. Two alternatives are worth mentioning.

Beautiful.ai bets on "smart templates" that automatically adapt the layout to the content you type in. The tool updated its editor in October 2025 and its template library in July 2026. It has a strong reputation for automatic visual consistency — if you want a polished result without touching any design settings, it's worth exploring.

Canva Magic Design also offers AI features for generating slides and content, with a generous free plan and thousands of templates. If you already use Canva for other visuals, the learning curve is basically zero.

Worth noting: Tome AI, which used to be a well-known alternative, shut down its presentation product around April 2025 to refocus on sales automation. I mention it because it still comes up in some articles — but if you see a recommendation for Tome for slides, it's outdated.

The AI presentation tool market moves fast. My recommendation: start with Gamma's free plan and see if it does the job before going further.

A few habits for better results

AI gives you a starting point. What you do with it after is what separates a decent presentation from one that actually convinces people.

Be specific in your prompt. "Make me a presentation about my business" gives you something generic. "Create an 8-slide deck to pitch my recruitment consulting service to industrial SMBs, with a problem slide, a solution slide, and a pricing slide" gives you something usable. Prompt engineering applies here just like everywhere else.

Review the generated content. AI structures things well, but it can produce vague phrasing or made-up numbers. Every factual claim you leave in the deck — you own that in front of your audience.

Match the tone to the audience. An investor pitch and an internal update don't speak the same language. Tell the tool upfront — it takes that into account.

Don't keep everything. AI tends to generate too much text per slide. A good presentation usually means fewer words, not more. Edit ruthlessly.

FAQ

Can AI really create a full presentation without any human input?

Largely, yes — but not entirely. Tools like Gamma AI generate a structure, text, and visuals from a prompt. The result is often presentable without major edits. But you're always responsible for fact-checking, adjusting the tone, and making sure it aligns with your actual message. AI handles the heavy lifting on formatting; the substance is still yours.

What does it cost to use Gamma AI or Microsoft Copilot for presentations?

Gamma offers a free plan with 400 lifetime AI credits, with each presentation typically using around 40 credits — so roughly ten free presentations. Beyond that, you need a paid plan for unlimited use. Microsoft Copilot in PowerPoint requires a Microsoft 365 Premium plan or an eligible business plan — there's no free tier for this feature.

Can I customize the presentations AI generates?

Yes. Both tools let you edit text, swap the visual theme, reorder slides, and adjust content after generation. Gamma has a web-based editor. Copilot works directly inside PowerPoint, giving you access to all the usual editing options in the app.

Can AI pull in my existing data or documents to build a presentation?

Yes — and that's actually one of the strongest use cases for these tools. Gamma can generate a presentation from an imported file. Copilot in PowerPoint can create slides from a Word document or a text brief. In both cases, the AI uses the material you provide to structure the content.

How private is my data when I use AI presentation tools?

That's a fair question, especially if you're working with sensitive data or client information. The terms vary by tool and change regularly. Before importing a confidential document, check the privacy policy of the tool you're using. As a general rule, enterprise tools like Microsoft 365 Copilot offer more formal contractual guarantees than consumer-facing tools.

Are AI-generated presentations actually "professional" and unique?

The visual output is often clean and consistent — well above what most people produce by hand without design training. On uniqueness, it's more nuanced: these tools rely on recurring templates and styles. Two people entering a similar prompt will get similar-looking decks. To stand out, focus on the precision of your prompt, your choice of theme, and above all the quality of the content you bring to it.

My takeaways

I'm done spending half a day nudging boxes around in PowerPoint. It's not about being lazy — it's about priorities. My time is worth more than layout work.

Gamma won me over for fast prototyping. Copilot is useful when I already have structured material to transform. Both have real limits — Gamma's PowerPoint export especially — but none of those limits justify going back to doing it by hand.

I test things on myself first. And here, the time savings are concrete enough that I can recommend it without hesitation. Try Gamma on a real presentation you have to do this week. Ten minutes, 40 credits, and you'll have your answer.
