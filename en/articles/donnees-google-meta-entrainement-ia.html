Your data
up against AI
by Google and Meta

You probably share a lot of your work life on Gmail, Google Photos, Instagram, or Facebook. I do too. And ever since Google and Meta started weaving AI into every corner of their services, one question keeps bugging me. Can my emails to a client, my product shots, or my posts end up training their models? I dug into it for you. Here is what I found, and more importantly, what you can actually do to limit the damage.

What you'll walk away with

  • You can unlink saving your history from Gemini training, starting November 18, 2026
  • Meta uses your public data to train Muse Spark, but Europeans can opt out using GDPR
  • On Instagram, one specific setting keeps your photos and Reels out of AI image generation

Why I dug into this

I spend most of my day bouncing between Gmail and Instagram. I get quotes, I stash project screenshots on Google Photos, I publish stuff on Facebook to test formats. Basically, I leave digital breadcrumbs everywhere.

And clearly, I'm not the only one feeling a little cheated. A 2026 Pew Research Center survey found 35% of respondents say they are more worried than excited about the growing use of AI. I am in that camp. Not because I fear technology. Because I want to stay in control of what I share.

Google's privacy policy was updated around October 2026. It now allows your emails, photos, and Drive files to be exploited for ad targeting via its AI, especially when AI tools are used with those services. At the same time, Google rolled out its Gemini 4 Carbon model on November 10, 2026. Meta is not sitting still. The company announced in mid-April 2025 that it would use European users' public data to train its AI models, with an effective date set for May 27, 2025. Today, its Meta AI assistant has been powered by the Muse Spark model since April 2026. It lives inside WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger.

Let's just say the risk is no longer theoretical. If you run a business, your mixed personal and professional data is a target. I suggest we walk through the settings that actually matter.

Google and your email

Google pushed an update on November 18, 2026. It lets you stop Gemini model training on your conversations without wiping your chat history. The idea is simple. Before, everything was tied together. If you wanted to stop Google from using your prompts to improve Gemini, you had to delete your history. Now the settings are split. You can keep your conversation history for yourself, while shutting off the training feed.

But watch out. This option only covers Gemini conversations. It does not fix everything. Google's privacy policy, updated around October 2026, still allows your emails, photos, and Drive files to be exploited for ad targeting via its AI, especially when AI tools are used with those services. That means your work emails sitting in Gmail, or the images you uploaded to Google Photos, can be analyzed to fine-tune AI-driven ad campaigns.

To break that link, head to your Google account's privacy settings. Look for the section dedicated to AI and activity management. The new thing is this clear split between saving your activity and using it for AI. Turn off the option that allows your data to be used to improve models. Keep your history if you need it for yourself.

Meta settings to check right now

Meta announced in mid-April 2025 that it planned to use European Facebook and Instagram users' public personal data to train its AI models. The effective date was set for May 27, 2025. Since then, the company has hit the gas hard. The Muse Spark model has powered the Meta AI assistant inside WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger since April 2026. Then on October 12, 2026, Meta introduced the Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full models, capable of handling complex tasks that require planning and reasoning.

In October 2026, the Meta AI app passed 10 million users in the United States and Canada. That is massive adoption. The more users there are, the more data flows around.

On Instagram, there is one precise setting that stops your public photos and Reels from being used for AI image generation. Go to Settings and activity, then Sharing and reuse. There, turn off the option Allow people to use your content on Instagram and with Meta AI features. This setting covers both posts and Reels.

On Facebook, the logic is similar for public content. Meta focuses on posts visible to everyone to train its models. The more open your profile is, the more exposed you are. If you use these networks to promote your business, you obviously post in public. The risk is real.

You can learn more about how Meta AI is integrated into WhatsApp and the other apps. And if you are curious about AI image generation, this article on how to create images with AI for free will shed light on the mechanics.

Your right to opt out in Europe

Users in the European Union and the United Kingdom have a valuable card up their sleeve. It is the right to object under Article 21 of the GDPR. This right lets you object to Meta using your public Facebook and Instagram posts for AI training. To do it, you fill out an objection form that Meta itself provides.

It is not a highly visible option. It is a legal right Meta was forced to set up. Whether you are in France, Belgium, Germany, or anywhere else in Europe, you can exercise it. The form applies to public posts. It does not cover private messages, which are off limits for this kind of harvesting under Meta's current policies.

Still, this right has limits. It applies geographically. If you have clients or an audience in the United States, know the legal framework is different. Over there, Meta passed 10 million users on its Meta AI app in October 2026, and the withdrawal mechanisms are less of a burden for the company.

To better understand the legal framework that protects your data in Europe, check out this breakdown of the AI Act for your business.

What I do beyond the settings

Changing settings is essential. It is not enough. I have a few simple habits to shrink my exposure surface.

I reread privacy policy updates when they drop. Google pushed one around October 2026. Meta does them regularly. These texts are long, but the dates and headings change. If you see the words artificial intelligence, model training, or advanced targeting pop up, it is time to check your settings.

I also limit what I put online in public mode. If a product photo or an email contains sensitive info, I think twice before posting it or storing it on a free account tied to an ad ecosystem.

Healthy skepticism

If a service is free, you are not the customer. You are the product. That is not a jab. It is a reminder. Every time Google or Meta rolls out a new free, built-in AI tool, check what you are allowing by default. The options change. I have proof: Google's November 2026 update finally split saving your history from model training.

FAQ

Can you completely turn off AI?

No. You cannot fully switch off AI at Google or Meta. You can limit model training on your personal data and block some reuse. But these companies have baked AI into the core of their services. Even if you cut off training, your data can still be analyzed for AI ad targeting, as Google's October 2026 policy update makes clear.

Do your private messages train their AI?

Public facts do not show that Meta uses your private messages to train Muse Spark or Muse Wonder. Meta focuses on public data for training, especially from European users. At Google, the situation is different when it comes to targeting. The October 2026 privacy policy allows your emails and Drive files to be exploited for AI ad targeting. For Gemini conversations, a separate setting has existed since November 18, 2026, to stop them from being used for training.

What happens if you do nothing?

If you change nothing, you implicitly accept the default terms. Google can exploit your emails, photos, and Drive files to fine-tune AI ad targeting. Meta can use your public posts to train its models, in Europe and elsewhere. Your Instagram photos and Reels can also be used for AI image generation if you leave that option on.

Same options in Europe and the United States?

No. Users in the European Union and the United Kingdom benefit from the right to object under Article 21 of the GDPR. This right lets them fill out an objection form against Meta using their public posts for AI training. This mechanism does not exist with the same strength outside Europe. In the United States and Canada, the Meta AI app has over 10 million users as of October 2026, but the control levers are less protective.

Can you erase past use of your data?

There is no dashboard that shows you exactly which data has already been used to train a model. You cannot download a history of exploitation. However, you can exercise your right to object for the future if you are in Europe. For the past, the only certainty is that changing your settings today does not claw back what has already been swallowed. That is why I prefer to act fast.

My takeaways

I do all of this first and foremost for myself. Because I refuse to find out six months from now that my product photos were used to generate visuals for a competitor, or that my strategic emails fed a targeting algorithm.

AI is a fabulous tool when you control it. It becomes a problem when it runs through you without clear consent. The settings exist. Google improved them in November 2026. Meta makes them available, sometimes hidden behind forms. It is up to you to turn them on. Ten minutes today means fewer regrets tomorrow.
