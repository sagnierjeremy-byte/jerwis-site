AI and money: the gap between the dream and reality

Here's what I see everywhere on social media right now: YouTube thumbnails with six-figure numbers, Instagram accounts selling "AI secrets" courses, LinkedIn threads where everyone claims to have automated everything and is living off passive income. And underneath it all, you — a curious entrepreneur, not a developer — wondering if you're missing out on something.

The short answer: no, you're not missing out on some hidden gold mine. But yes, there are real opportunities to grab — if you know where to look.

What strikes me is the gap between expectations and measured reality. According to an analysis of 200 AI projects in France conducted between 2022 and 2025, the median ROI for SMBs reached 159%, with a return on investment achieved in an average of 6.7 months. That's solid. But an MIT study published in summer 2025 tempers the enthusiasm: 95% of AI projects in SMBs fail and have no tangible financial impact. And in April 2026, only 20% of French organizations reported actually generating growth through AI, while 74% were hoping to.

Two figures that seem to contradict each other, but actually tell the same story: AI can pay off, but not on its own. You need a method, a precise angle, and a real understanding of what AI is genuinely good at.

AI is a lever. Not a magic wand. It lets you deliver faster, serve more clients, create more content — but it doesn't replace work, it amplifies it. That's the fundamental distinction that most dream-sellers conveniently leave out.

What actually pays off

So, concretely — where do you start if you're not a developer and you want to use AI to generate income?

Automation for small businesses. This is probably the most solid path right now. Small and medium businesses have repetitive tasks that eat up enormous amounts of time: sorting emails, generating quotes, summarizing meetings, following up with clients. Setting up systems that automate all of that — with tools like Make or AI agents — is genuine added value that businesses are willing to pay for. And you don't need to code to do it. I wrote a full article on the topic if you want to go deeper: automating your tasks with AI without code.

Content creation at scale. Writing articles, scripting videos, generating visuals: AI speeds all of this up dramatically. The important nuance is that the value doesn't come from 100% automated content. An article generated entirely without editing or a personal angle — it shows, and it doesn't convert. The human touch is still what separates useful content from noise. AI saves you time on structure and first drafts; you bring the perspective, the experience, the consistency.

AI consulting and prompt engineering. Companies are looking for people who can drive AI tools to automate their strategies or make sense of their data. You don't need to be an engineer: you need to understand how to write good instructions (what's called prompt engineering), know which tool to use for which job, and be able to guide a team through adoption. It's a skill that monetizes well, and it's accessible. If you want to understand how to write good prompts, I have a dedicated article: writing a good prompt when you're not a developer.

Digital products and websites. AI also lets you turn ideas into products faster than before — websites, simple apps, downloadable resources. It's not magic either, but the time between idea and launch has genuinely shrunk.

My takeaways

The methods that work all share one thing: they build on a skill or knowledge you already have, and AI amplifies it. If you're starting from zero on a topic, AI doesn't fill that gap — it accelerates what you already know how to do.

The promises to run from

Now let's talk about what doesn't hold up. And there's a lot of it.

"Total passive income with AI." The idea that an AI agent can generate money while you sleep, with zero input from you, is sold everywhere. In the rare cases where it works, it's the result of considerable upfront work — building the system, testing it, fixing it, maintaining it. That's not passive, it's deferred.

Trading bots "powered by AI." This is one of the most widespread financial scams of 2026. Fake ads, influencers cloned via deepfake "recommending" platforms, promises of guaranteed returns. AI is used here not to create value, but to make the fraud more convincing. This type of scam has been industrialized.

$997 courses that promise to make you an "AI expert" in a weekend. Some are honest and useful. Many are riding the wave by selling recycled content and unverifiable promises. The red flag: when the course promises a specific income in a specific timeframe, no conditions attached.

How do you spot a scam? A few simple signals:

  • A specific, guaranteed income promise → red flag.
  • A testimonial with a blurred face or a synthetic voice → possible deepfake, learn to spot them here.
  • A "turnkey system" that requires zero prior skill → be suspicious.
  • Artificial urgency ("offer valid for 24 hours") → classic pressure tactic.

The scams of 2026 are harder to detect than before, because AI now lets anyone write phishing messages without spelling errors, imitate well-known voices, or simulate faces on video. The visual or audio credibility of a message is no longer a guarantee of its authenticity.

The trap to avoid

When you see an influencer hyping an AI investment platform with jaw-dropping numbers, first check whether the video is recent, whether the account has been active for a long time, and whether any independent sources mention that platform. A well-made deepfake can use a real person's face and voice without their consent.

How to make this sustainable

Having access to the tools is one thing. Building a profitable activity around AI is another.

What I see in the people who pull it off is that they start from a real problem — their own or their clients' — and use AI to solve it faster or better than before. Not the other way around. It's not "I have an AI tool, let's find a problem" — it's "I have this recurring problem, can AI help me here?"

The other point is transparency. Using AI to produce content or services is perfectly legitimate. Claiming everything is 100% human when it isn't is a stance that erodes trust over time. Clients and audiences are increasingly able to detect fully automated content — and they penalize it.

Finally, continuous learning remains central. AI moves fast. A tool you master today will be replaced or updated in six months. What doesn't change is your ability to learn, adapt, and understand what you're doing. That's the real barrier to entry — not code, not technical skill, but active curiosity.

FAQ

Do you need to be a developer to make money with AI?

No. The most accessible paths — automation for SMBs, content creation, AI consulting — don't require knowing how to code. What matters is understanding how the tools work, how to write good instructions, and how to identify the right problems to solve.

What initial investment do you need to get started with AI?

It depends on the angle you choose. Many tools have a free version that's good enough for testing. Paid subscriptions to advanced models (ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro, etc.) generally run a few dozen dollars a month. The main investment is your learning time.

How do you choose the right AI tools for your business?

Start from the problem you want to solve, not the tool. Then test the free versions before paying. And be wary of comparisons that are too old: versions change fast. I have an article to help you choose the right tool for your use case.

Can AI really generate passive income?

Partially. It's possible to build systems that run semi-autonomously, but they require significant upfront work and regular maintenance. The idea of totally passive income with zero effort — the way it's sold on social media — doesn't match reality.

How do I protect my business from AI-based scams?

A few simple habits: always verify someone's identity through an independent channel if something feels off. Be suspicious of guaranteed return promises. Learn to spot deepfakes and content generated to deceive — I have a dedicated article: spotting AI-generated content or a deepfake.

Will AI replace my job or create new opportunities?

Both, depending on the situation. Some repetitive tasks will indeed be automated. But new roles are emerging — AI consulting, custom automation, augmented content creation — that didn't exist three years ago. The question isn't "will AI replace me?" but "how do I reposition myself with AI?"

My takeaways

I do all of this for myself first. Understanding where the real opportunities are and where the traps are is what keeps me from wasting time and money on empty promises.

What I've taken away from everything I've read and tested: AI is a powerful lever for people who already know what they're doing and are looking to do it better or faster. It doesn't replace expertise, your network, or the trust you've built with your clients. But in the hands of someone who understands their market, it can make a real difference.

The right starting point isn't searching for "how to make money with AI." It's looking at what you already do, identifying what costs you the most time, and asking whether AI can help you there, specifically.
