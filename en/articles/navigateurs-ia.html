Browsing with
built-in AI
actually useful or just hype?

You've probably heard about browsers that bake AI right into the toolbar. No more switching to a ChatGPT tab, the assistant is right there, on the page, while you work. But does that actually change anything concrete for you as a non-technical entrepreneur? I dug into what Perplexity Comet and OpenAI Atlas really offer, what they delivered on, and where they fell short. Here's what I took away.

What "AI browser" actually means

For a long time, a web browser was just a pipe. You typed an address, a page loaded. If you needed help, you opened another tab with ChatGPT or Perplexity, copy-pasted your content, then switched back. Clunky, but it worked.

AI-native browsers break that pattern. The assistant lives inside the browser itself. It sees the page you're reading, it can summarize an article while you scroll through it, draft an email based on web content, or run several actions across different sites in sequence, without you having to manage each step manually.

That's what's called an agentic workflow: the AI doesn't just answer a one-off question, it chains steps together to reach a goal you've set for it.

For an entrepreneur juggling prospecting, research, writing, and business tools, that's a promise worth taking seriously. But a promise is just a promise until you look at what's actually happening.

Comet: Perplexity's browser

Perplexity launched Comet in July 2025 for Windows and macOS. The browser is built on Chromium, the same foundation as Chrome. That means your usual extensions work, and the learning curve is basically zero if you already use Chrome.

What Comet adds is the Perplexity search engine baked directly into the browser. You can ask it to summarize the article you're reading, describe an image on the page, run a topic search without leaving your current context, or draft an email from web content. To do all that, it draws on several language models, including GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 depending on the task.

If you've never used Perplexity, I'd suggest reading my article on what Perplexity is before going further. It helps explain why this integration makes sense.

In February 2026, Perplexity also launched Perplexity Computer, an AI agent capable of handling multi-step tasks autonomously. Comet and Computer work together as an ecosystem: the browser as the interface, the agent as the hands.

The Android version arrived in November 2025, iOS in March 2026. The full offering is now available across all major platforms.

My takeaway on Comet

Comet's strength is keeping you in your workflow. You don't open an extra tool: you stay on your page and ask the AI to act on it. For research or quick drafting, that's a genuine gain in fluidity. But if you want to get more out of it, knowing how to phrase your requests makes all the difference, and this article on prompts for non-developers will help you start on the right foot.

Atlas: a short run, but an instructive one

OpenAI unveiled ChatGPT Atlas on October 21, 2025 for macOS, with Windows, iOS, and Android versions announced. The idea was similar to Comet: a browser built around the ChatGPT interface, with AI woven into every browsing action.

But Atlas didn't last. On August 9, 2026, OpenAI officially shut the project down. The agentic features Atlas offered were absorbed directly into ChatGPT and Codex.

That decision says something. OpenAI apparently concluded that a standalone browser wasn't the right architecture. Better to fold those capabilities into the tool people already use than to ask them to switch browsers.

You can find a full history of what Atlas offered in the dedicated glossary entry.

What this trajectory teaches us is that the market isn't settled yet. Players are testing formats. Some stick, some don't. For you, that means adopting an AI browser too early could mean starting over from scratch a few months later.

What this actually changes day to day

AI browsers can automate multi-step tasks, summarize content, answer contextual questions, and interact with websites to reach a goal you've set. That's what research on these tools has documented since early 2026.

In practice, for an entrepreneur, it might look like this:

  • You're reading a deep-dive article on a competitor. You ask the browser to pull out the key points and write a three-sentence summary for your team.
  • You're looking at a commercial offer in PDF form on a website. You ask the AI to compare the important points with another document you have open in another tab.
  • You want to send a follow-up email to a prospect. You ask the AI to draft that message based on the contact page you're looking at.

These are real gains. Not dramatic ones, but stacked up over a week, they lighten the collection and reformulation work that eats time without really creating value.

The limit is that these features still require you to know what to ask. The AI built into the browser isn't magic. It's only as good as the way you talk to it. And that's where knowing how to automate without code becomes a real edge.

Do you actually need to leave Chrome?

Chrome held 69.39% of global browser market share in August 2026, well ahead of Safari at 15.83% and Edge at 5.36%. Those numbers speak for themselves: Chrome is what the vast majority of people use, and Google isn't standing still on AI.

Google launched Gemini Agent in September 2026, an experimental tool for multi-step tasks, available on the web for Ultra subscribers. Gemini is already integrated into Chrome via Google Search and AI Overviews. If you want to understand how Google is weaving AI into its search, this article on AI Overviews in France gives a solid overview.

In other words, Chrome isn't static. It's gradually absorbing AI features, even if for now they're less deep than what Comet offers.

The real question isn't "Chrome or Comet?" It's "what for?"

If you spend a lot of time doing research, summarizing content, or drafting from web pages, Comet can give you something Chrome doesn't natively offer yet. If you mainly use your browser to access SaaS tools, manage email, and join online meetings, the gain will be much harder to feel.

The trap to avoid

Switching browsers has an invisible cost: your bookmarks, your extensions, your saved passwords, your habits. That cost is real and easy to underestimate. Before migrating, test Comet alongside Chrome for two weeks on one specific use case. If you don't notice a concrete difference on that use case, stay where you are.

Privacy: the topic people skip too fast

AI browsers need access to your browsing data to function. And that data can be sent to third-party cloud services. This is documented, and it's the business model behind these tools.

For personal, general-purpose use, that's often fine. For an entrepreneur browsing client data, confidential commercial offers, or sensitive documents, it's a question worth asking before you adopt the tool.

That's not a reason to reject everything. It's a reason to know what you're running in which browser. Using Comet for your public research and Chrome for your sensitive business tools is a reasonable approach.

The models powering all of this

Comet draws on several models depending on the task, including GPT-4o and Claude 3.5. The model landscape is moving fast: Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 on September 1, 2026, with Fable 5.1 being its most capable model for complex reasoning and long-running agents. OpenAI launched GPT-6 Astra in early September 2026 for a limited number of organizations, with broader availability planned for Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise subscribers.

What that means for you: AI browsers will keep improving at the pace of the models feeding them. What Comet does today will be more capable in six months, without you necessarily having to switch tools.

That's good news. And it's also a reason not to wait for "the perfect version" before you start exploring.

FAQ

What exactly is an AI-native browser for an entrepreneur?

It's a standard web browser with an AI assistant added that can interact with the pages you visit. It can summarize an article, draft an email from web content, answer questions about what you're reading, or run several actions across different sites on your behalf. The difference from an AI extension or a ChatGPT tab is that the assistant is inside your browsing flow, not alongside it.

What real productivity gains can I expect from an AI browser?

The gains are real but hard to predict in advance: they depend on how you work. Research, content summarization, and quick drafting are where the gain is most noticeable. If you spend a lot of time copy-pasting information between tabs and reformulating content, you'll feel a difference. If you mainly use your browser to access SaaS tools, the gain will be more limited.

Are AI browsers secure and do they respect the privacy of my professional data?

That's the main thing to watch. These browsers need access to your browsing data and may send it to third-party cloud servers. For sensitive data (clients, commercial offers, confidential documents), it's better to keep uses separate: AI browser for public research, standard browser for sensitive business tools. Read the terms of use before adopting any tool on a professional machine.

Will Chrome integrate enough AI that I won't need to switch browsers?

Google already integrates AI into Chrome via Gemini and AI Overviews in search. In September 2026, Google launched Gemini Agent, an experimental multi-step task tool, for Ultra subscribers. Chrome remains dominant at 69.39% of global market share. Google's trajectory points toward more built-in AI, but the pace and depth of that integration are still worth watching.

How do I choose the right AI browser for my needs as a non-developer entrepreneur?

Start by identifying your most repetitive browsing tasks: research, summarization, drafting, searching. Test Comet on those specific tasks for two weeks alongside Chrome. If you notice a real gain in fluidity on those use cases, adopting it makes sense. If you don't see a difference, stay on Chrome and keep an eye on how it evolves.

Can I use an AI browser for specific tasks and keep Chrome for everything else?

Yes, and that's actually the approach I'd recommend. Nothing forces you to migrate entirely. Comet is built on Chromium just like Chrome, so the learning curve is minimal. You can easily use Comet for your research and drafting, and keep Chrome for your business tools, email, and anything touching sensitive data. That way you get the benefits without taking on the risks.

My takeaways

An AI-native browser isn't a gimmick, but it's not an immediate revolution for everyone either. Perplexity Comet is the most polished option right now. OpenAI Atlas showed that you can test something and pull the plug fast in this space.

For me, the real question isn't "which browser?" but "for what?" I do all of this for myself first: understanding these tools before they become unavoidable, testing them on real use cases, keeping what works. And what works is using the right tool for the right context, not migrating everything at once just because something is new.

If you want to start somewhere, start with Comet for your research. And keep Chrome for everything else, at least for now.
